# Vigil

Bushfire spread prediction for Victoria, with a public interactive map. Portfolio project by Rishi (first-year data science, RMIT).

## How to work with Rishi
- Claude writes the code. Rishi makes the design decisions and must be able to defend them in an interview.
- At each real design choice: explain the options in a few lines, give a recommendation, and let Rishi decide. Record the decision in the Decisions section below.
- Keep chat replies short. Rishi doesn't read long docs or READMEs, so put the important part first.
- Give genuine pushback. Don't just agree.
- Plumbing (downloads, glue, config) needs no discussion. Just do it well.

## Plan
1. **Data pipeline:** group NASA FIRMS VIIRS 375 m detections (2012 onwards) into fire events and build daily burnt-area maps. Align inputs: BARRA2 weather and wind, DEM (slope, aspect), fuel type, DEA fuel moisture.
2. **Physics baseline:** a cellular automaton using CSIRO spread models (dry eucalypt forest, grassland).
3. **ML model:** a U-Net predicting the next-day fire map (following Huot et al. 2022, "Next Day Wildfire Spread"). Split by fire season (labelled by the year it ends): test 2020 (Black Summer, untouched). Model selection by leave-one-season-out cross-validation over 2013–2019 and 2021–2025; final model trained on all 12. See Decisions.
4. **Compare:** physics vs ML vs hybrid (physics output as an ML input). Metrics: IoU, precision/recall/F1 on newly burnt cells.
5. **Website:** a map where you click to start a hypothetical fire (pick the weather), or replay a real fire, and compare model predictions with what actually burnt.

## Decisions
- 2026-11-05: Dropped the ignition risk model. Spread prediction is the headline.
- 2026-11-05: BARRA2 replaces ERA5 (finer resolution, no queue).
- 2026-11-05: BARRA-C2 (4 km) over BARRA-R2 (12 km): 12 km smooths out terrain-driven wind in the mountains (e.g. East Gippsland). Hourly, so wind changes keep their timing.
- 2026-11-05: Weather for fire-season months only (Oct–Apr). DEECA 2012–2025: 11 of 288 bushfires (2.3% of area) started outside that window.
- 2026-11-05: Download VIIRS from both S-NPP and NOAA-20, but build fire maps from S-NPP only. Adding NOAA-20 from 2018 would roughly double overpasses, so detection rates would differ between train (2012–18) and test (2019–20).
- 2026-11-05: Don't clip fires at the state border; keep cross-border fires whole in modelling data (fire doesn't stop at the state line). The ABS Victoria outline (`data/raw/abs_boundary`, STE 2021) is only for sanity checks against Vic-reported totals: season 2020 inside Vic = 1.417 Mha from pieces ≥50 ha, plus ~0.1 Mha in small pieces, so ≈1.5 Mha ✓. Fire area = merged (unioned) outline per fire.
- 2026-11-05: Season split. Test = 2020 (Black Summer). Validation = 2019, chosen as the season most like the test: 97% of its 176 kha is east of 145°E (2020: 99%), median elevation 590 m (2020: 523 m), 71% on slopes >10° (2020: 49%), so mountain forest like 2020. It's also the season right before the test, which is standard for time-ordered data. Rejected 2025: 95% western grass/heath, so tuning on it would pick models for the wrong landscape. Runner-up was 2013 (63% east). Train = 2013–2018 and 2021–2025 (~1,000 kha, ~370 kha of it in the east, mostly 2013–14). Season 2012 dropped: VIIRS starts 2012-01-20 and BARRA has nothing before Jan 2012, so about half the season is missing (only 3 kha anyway). Including seasons after the test season is deliberate: spread physics doesn't change between years, and training data is thin.
- 2026-11-05: FIRMS filters (S-NPP, 712,447 detections; code in `src/vigil/events.py`): drop `type` ≠ 0 (−10,590), low confidence (−36,463; 4.5% of 2020 bushfire detections, within 1.8–9.3% for other seasons, so no test-season bias), persistent hot spots (−9,117), outside seasons 2013–2025 (−25,344). Kept 630,933. Persistent hot spot = ~1 km cell (0.01°) with ≥10 distinct days across ≥5 seasons, never removing a detection inside a same-season DEECA bushfire outline. 5 seasons, not 3, because East Gippsland forest burnt in both 2014 and 2020 reaches 10+ days over 3 seasons. It catches Hastings steelworks, Longford, Laverton, Yallourn, an Orbost site (40 days/9 seasons, never inside a bushfire) and NSW Riverina stubble burns (Nov–Dec, Apr–May peaks). FIRMS `type` alone misses all of these.
- 2026-11-05: Event grouping is tuned against DEECA bushfires (≥5 detections): every fire should keep ≥95% of its detections in one event (not 100%, because DEECA records some complexes of separate ignitions as one fire). Pieces more than 30 days from a fire's main event are ignored: they're later burns inside the outline, not a split.
- 2026-11-05: Event thresholds: link detections within **5 km and 14 days** (`python -m vigil.events build --dist 5 --gap 14`). The 14-day gap is needed because smoke and cloud hid the 2020 fires for over a week. 8 of 250 DEECA fires stay below 95%: 2 complexes of separate ignitions burning at the same time 11–37 km apart (W20141735, W202513017), 1 mixed (W201634025), and 5 small late flare-ups 11–29 days later; none are in 2019 or 2020. Larger distances fix only 3 more fires, but the share of detections in bushfire-tagged events that lie outside DEECA bushfire outlines goes from 12% (5 km) to 53% (15 km). After linking, detections more than 30 days outside an event's main activity (the span holding its middle 95% of detections) become their own events (only 4 moved). Output: `data/processed/fire_events.parquet`, 47,416 events.
- 2026-11-05: Cut training windows around each day's active fire, not the whole event.
- 2026-11-05: Modelling events = DEECA bushfire events + unmatched events with ≥2 active days and ≥11 detections, with the same rules for train, val and test (so NSW Black Summer fires, unmatched because DEECA covers Vic only, are in the test set). Excluded: planned burns (lit in mild weather and kept under control, so their spread isn't bushfire spread; 1,408 events), small unmatched events (mostly farm burns, and no next-day pair; 41,668), and the 65 events that run across a season boundary into or out of val/test (1,521 detections, 3 of them DEECA bushfires, largest 266 detections). Those are dropped, not split by day: splitting by day would put a fire's early days in one split and its later days in another, which leaks. Each event's split is the season of its first detection. Column `exclude_reason` in `fire_events.parquet`.
- 2026-11-05: Modelling set after event rules (superseded by the label decisions below): train 3,658 events / 235k detections, val 269 / 26k, test 348 / 203k.
- Known limitation: 2020 had extreme fire behaviour (pyroconvection, long-range spotting) rarely seen in the training seasons, so the test checks how well the model holds up on conditions more extreme than it trained on.
- Earlier, from the benched learning build, still relevant: work out terrain features at full DEM resolution, then summarise to the coarser grid (never average elevation first). Summarise slope as the fraction of each cell above slope thresholds, with thresholds picked from a histogram.

- 2026-11-05: Fuel type = CSIRO BFC national fuel map. It's the only easy-to-get source that's consistent across Vic, NSW and SA (the test set includes NSW Black Summer fires), and it links to fire behaviour models. DEECA/AFDRS layers are Vic-only and behind a login; NVIS is vegetation, not fuel (kept as the fallback).
- 2026-11-05: Time since last burnt (`src/vigil/burn_history.py`, built on the 375 m VicGrid grid set in `config.py`). Years since last burnt, capped at 10 ("10+"). Previous seasons come from all sources: DEECA (Vic), NPWS (NSW, `data/raw/fire_history_nsw`) and DEW (SA, `data/raw/fire_history_sa`, stale after 2020-02) polygons, plus FIRMS footprints; polygons use season only. The current season adds FIRMS burns up to the day before the prediction day (local fire day, `LOCAL_TZ`); that's known on the day, so it isn't leakage. The current fire's own earlier burn is excluded here and goes in a separate channel instead: the current fire's cumulative burnt area up to the prediction day (planned, not built). Planned burns count as burns. Ablation kept: `tslb(..., source="combined" | "firms")`.
- Finding: FIRMS footprints (one 375 m cell per detection) catch only 49% of DEECA's 2020 Black Summer burnt area, because fire fronts move between overpasses and smoke and cloud hide them. So FIRMS-only time since burnt is porous, and so will be any daily burnt-area map built from detections alone.

- 2026-11-05: Time since last burnt now defaults to MCD64A1 for previous seasons (`tslb(..., source="mcd64")`), because it's the same product in every state. Burnt within the previous 10 seasons, season 2013: Vic 9.4% / NSW 7.3% (state polygons gave 10.5% / 2.2%). It catches 83% of DEECA 2020 bushfire cells. "combined" and "firms" are kept for the ablation. The current season still comes from FIRMS up to the day before.
- Label findings (2026-11-05, season 2020, `src/vigil/labels.py`): interpolating burn day inside MCD64A1 outlines from the nearest VIIRS detection gets 0.9 days mean error on hidden cells (91% within 1 day); IDW-8 is no better. Plain nearest picks up later burning inside outlines (Corryong median 26 Mar); limiting it to detections between MCD64A1 date −2 and +3 days fixes this (cells more than 7 days off: 4.4% → 0.8%). Documented arrivals (Mallacoota 31 Dec, Buchan/Sarsfield/Clifton Creek overnight 30–31 Dec) come out 1 day late, because the first detection is the ~02:00 night overpass. Coverage of modelling events: 2,532 have no MCD64A1 area and 595 have little (mostly train, 102k detections). For events with an outline, detections more than 750 m outside it are 6% in test but 40% in train, mostly cropland (62% missed) and grass (67%). 1,246 train events (85k detections, 36% of train detections) are unmatched events mostly on cropland, peaking Mar–May: likely autumn stubble burns.

- 2026-11-05: Labels (`python -m vigil.labels build` → `data/processed/labels.nc`, `label_events.parquet`), following Parks 2014 day-of-burning. Fire outline = connected MCD64A1 burnt patch. Burn day for each outline cell = nearest VIIRS S-NPP detection dated within the cell's MCD64A1 date −2…+3 days (plain nearest if none); the cell's event = that detection's event. Daily labels for prediction day D: burnt so far = burn_day ≤ D; newly burnt = burn_day = D+1.
- 2026-11-05: Fire days run 06:00–06:00 local (`FIRE_DAY_START_HOUR` in `config.py`), so the ~02:00 night overpass counts toward the day whose fire it shows. With calendar dates, documented arrivals came out 1 day late. Now: Mallacoota 31 Dec ✓, Buchan 30 Dec ✓, Clifton Creek 30 Dec ✓, Sarsfield 31 Dec–1 Jan, Corryong earliest 31 Dec. Used by time since burnt too.
- 2026-11-05: Unmatched events with >50% of detections on CSIRO "Croplands" (class 640) are dropped as stubble burns (1,329 events; peak Mar–May). Asymmetry: DEECA bushfires on cropland are kept, so real Victorian crop and grass fires stay while real NSW/SA ones (always "unmatched") are dropped.
- 2026-11-05: Events with no MCD64A1 area (2,030) or MCD64A1 cells < 0.5× their detected cells (368) are dropped, so train and test labels are both outline-based. Uncertain mask (`uncertain_day`, excluded from the loss on day D when 0 < uncertain_day ≤ D+1): detection cells outside any MCD64A1 outline (instead of labelling them burnt), cells of excluded events, and MCD64A1 patches with no detections (so unlabelled burning never counts as "didn't burn").
- 2026-11-05: Final label set: train 459 events / 83k detections / 98k burnt cells (1.38 Mha) / 2,024 event-days with new burn; val 24 / 15k / 10k cells (0.14 Mha) / 144 event-days; test 65 / 197k / 193k cells (2.72 Mha) / 453 event-days. Validation is thin (144 event-days), so report it with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Known limitation: after these filters the ML model has seen very few grass and crop fires (cropland rule, and MCD64A1 misses most grass/crop burning). The website should handle grassland ignitions with the physics model, or show a warning.

- 2026-11-05: Grid = 375 m in VicGrid94 (EPSG:3111), 2,300 × 1,660 cells over our bounds (`GRID_RES_M`). It matches the VIIRS pixel that sets the timing, and it's finer than MCD64A1 (463 m) and BARRA-C2 (4 km). Terrain and fuel (~90–107 m) are summarised up to it.
- 2026-11-05: Validation = leave-one-season-out cross-validation over all 12 non-test seasons (2013–2019, 2021–2025), replacing the single 2019 validation season. Why: after label filtering, 2019 had only 144 event-days (24 events), too few to choose models reliably. Each fold holds out one season; report the mean and spread across folds, weighted by event-days (fold sizes range from 75 event-days in 2022 to 293 in 2013) and unweighted, plus the 2019 fold on its own since it's the season most like the test. The final model is trained on all 12 seasons. 2020 stays the untouched test set. Seasons are whole fires, so no fire is split across folds; events spanning seasons in different folds (or into/out of test) are dropped (see the 2026-11-05 cross-fold fix below).

- 2026-11-05: Daily spread (`data/processed/daily_spread.csv`): the furthest newly burnt cell is a median 2.1 km (train) / 2.2 km (test) from burnt-so-far, but the tail differs: p99 22.6 km vs 51.9 km; newly burnt cells >24 km away are 0.5% of train vs 5.8% of test (Black Summer runs, plus spot fires and new ignitions pulled into events).
- 2026-11-05: Windows (`python -m vigil.windows` → `data/processed/windows.parquet`): 128 × 128 cells (48 km), centred on each day's active fire (cells with burn_day = D); large fires get one window per 64 × 64 block of active fire. A sample needs active fire on day D. Windows cover 97.9% of next-day new burn in train (91% in test). Held-out seasons and the test set are scored on whole fires (the U-Net takes any input size), with metrics split by distance from burnt-so-far: ≤6, 6–24, >24 km.
- 2026-11-05: Window centres are jittered randomly by up to ±16 cells, freshly each epoch in the loader (`window_origin(..., rng)`), so fires aren't always centred.
- 2026-11-05: The sampling rate of no-new-burn samples is a hyperparameter tuned in the season cross-validation (keep all / 50% / 25%, `keep_no_burn`), not matched to the test set's rate (that would peek at test). Shares by window: 49% across the 12 CV seasons vs 10% in test. (The earlier 70% vs 34% counted every day between a fire's first and last burn, including days with no active fire.)
- 2026-11-05: Samples per fold (windows / with new burn / no new burn): 2013 477/294/183, 2014 350/245/105, 2015 228/74/154, 2016 126/38/88, 2017 210/77/133, 2018 325/126/199, 2019 224/156/68, 2021 395/226/169, 2022 176/95/81, 2023 164/66/98, 2024 280/113/167, 2025 368/187/181; CV total 3,323 / 1,697 / 1,626 (2,502 at 50% no-burn, 2,103 at 25%). Test 1,977 / 1,776 / 201.

- 2026-11-05: Cross-validation uses 5 folds of whole seasons instead of 12 single-season folds (some seasons had under 80 windows with new burn): fold 0 = 2013+2023, 1 = 2014+2016+2017, 2 = 2015+2019+2022, 3 = 2018+2025, 4 = 2021+2024 (`CV_FOLDS` in `config.py`). Balanced on windows with new burn (313–360 per fold; 628–693 windows), with the three biggest/most test-like seasons (2013, 2014, 2019) in different folds; found by exhaustive search. Report the mean ± spread over the 5 folds; final model on all 12 seasons.

- 2026-11-05: Weather inputs (`python -m vigil.weather` → `data/processed/weather_daily.nc`, on the BARRA grid; 2,957 fire days). For fire day D and D+1 (06:00–06:00 local): max 10 m wind speed; u/v wind at the hour of peak fire danger (the hour maximising FFDI's weather terms, −0.0345 RH + 0.0338 T + 0.0234 V, with the drought factor held constant); max temperature; min RH clipped at 100. Resampled bilinearly to the 375 m grid per window, and winds rotated from true east/north into VicGrid axes (up to ~3°).
- 2026-11-05: Inputs (`src/vigil/dataset.py`), 28 channels: active_D, own_burnt (this fire up to D), tslb/10, elevation, grad_x/grad_y, slope_gt5/15/25 (thresholds from the land slope histogram: 62% of land is under 2°; 5/15/25° are exceeded by ~24/7/1.5%), 9 fuel-group fractions (`static.py`), 5 weather × {D, D+1}. Target = this fire's cells newly burnt on D+1. Loss mask also excludes other modelling fires burning by D+1 in the window. Wind and aspect stay as vector components; rot90/flip augmentation rotates them consistently (`tests/test_augment.py`). Vector components are normalised with mean 0 and a shared scale, so augmentation stays valid after normalisation. Normalisation stats come from training folds only (`norm_stats.json`: one set per CV fold plus "all" for the final model).
- 2026-11-05: **Scope rule: fire season only.** A window needs D and D+1 both in October–April, the same rule in every split, including test (`SEASON_MONTHS` in `windows.py`). This dropped 655 windows (mostly unmatched autumn/winter fires, 82 from DEECA bushfires; 37 in test). BARRA covers Oct–Apr only; the 8 windows with D = 1 Oct also lack complete weather and are dropped by `Data.has_weather`. Windows now: folds 0–4 = 491/586/500/545/583 (282–334 with new burn), test 1,940.
- Known limitation: weather for D+1 is BARRA reanalysis of the actual day, which assumes a perfect forecast. Real-time use would rely on forecasts and be less accurate.

- 2026-11-05: Example-window check (2014-02-08, East Gippsland): the target patches southwest of the main fire are real, not interpolation. 89% of those cells have the fire's own D+1 detection within 1 km (1,250 detections, 9 Feb 15:10 and 10 Feb 02:31), MCD64A1 agrees (+1 day), and the fire already had 41 detections there on D (small fires visible in active_D). They're a median 6.8 km (max 15 km) from burnt-so-far, not 30–40 km.
- 2026-11-05: In training, new-burn cells more than 24 km from the fire's burnt area on D are masked from the loss as unpredictable new ignitions (`mask_far`; distance to the whole fire, using a 24 km margin around the window). Share of new-burn cells this covers: fold 0 0.00%, 1 0.02%, 2 1.79%, 3 0.28%, 4 0.99% (test would be 4.94%). Inside actual windows it's smaller (fold 2: 0.10%). Evaluation keeps these cells and scores them in the >24 km band.
- 2026-11-05: Dryness inputs (`python -m vigil.dryness` → `dryness_daily.nc`): KBDI (metric Keetch–Byram; 5.08 mm interception per run of rain days; mean annual rain per cell from SILO 2000–2025) and McArthur drought factor (Griffiths 1999, with a 20-day rain-event window and the smallest x of the events), from SILO daily rain and max temp, the same method for every year (spin-up from 2000). Channels kbdi_D, df_D (30 channels in total). SILO rain for date D runs to 9 am D, so it's known on D. Checks: median KBDI 125 on 30 Dec 2019 and 132 on 8 Feb 2014; 0 in wet Nov 2022.
- Note: the drought factor doesn't change the peak-danger hour. It's one value per cell per day, so its ln(DF) term is the same at every hour and the hourly argmax of FFDI is unchanged. If DF should affect fire danger directly, the option is a peak-FFDI channel for D and D+1 (not added; Rishi to decide).

- 2026-11-05: Peak McArthur FFDI for D and D+1 added as channels (32 in total): 2·exp(−0.45 + 0.987 ln DF + max hourly weather terms), using that day's drought factor (`danger_max` in `weather_daily.nc`).
- 2026-11-05: Evaluation (`src/vigil/evaluate.py`): season CV over the 5 folds, scored on whole fires (fire extent + 24 km margin), excluding cells already burnt and the loss mask, with distance bands ≤6 / 6–24 / >24 km. Main metric: F1 on newly burnt cells, pooled per held-out fold, threshold tuned on the other folds (IoU = F1/(2−F1), so it ranks models identically; reported alongside). Also PR-AUC (threshold-free).
- Huot et al. 2022 (arXiv 2112.02447), for reference: US 2012–2020, 1 km, 64 × 64 windows, 18,545 samples, MODIS active-fire target at t+1, uncertain pixels ignored in the loss. Test PR-AUC / precision / recall (%), Table II, checked against the PDF: neural network 28.4 / 33.6 / 43.1, random forest 22.5 / 26.3 / 46.9, logistic regression 19.8 / 32.5 / 35.3, persistence 11.5 / 35.7 / 27.3 (no F1/IoU reported). Deep model: weighted cross-entropy with weight 3 on fire. LR/RF: 3×3 neighbourhood features, no-fire weight W = 7 (LR) / 5 (RF), RF max depth 15. Not directly comparable to ours: their target is all active fire at t+1 (including pixels already burning on t), ours is newly burnt cells only.

- 2026-11-05: Baseline results (5-fold season CV, mean ± sd; `data/processed/baseline_cv.csv`). Persistence from all burnt area: F1 0.105 ± 0.047, PR-AUC 0.039. Persistence from active fire on D: **F1 0.178 ± 0.073, PR-AUC 0.081 ± 0.043**. Wind-shifted, from all burnt area: F1 0.098. Wind-shifted, from active fire: F1 0.182 ± 0.076, PR-AUC 0.082. Tuning always picks the tightest setting (k = 1 cell; wind smear 0.25 cells per m/s), and every baseline scores 0 beyond 6 km. Wind is correctly oriented but weak: new burn lies downwind 58% of the time (88% on the 8 days with peak wind > 8 m/s). Fold 4 (2021+2024) is much lower (F1 0.06) than the others (0.16–0.24). Active-fire persistence is the baseline to beat. (Superseded: these numbers are from the old folds; see the rerun below.)

- 2026-11-05: Physics baseline plan approved. Cellular automaton on the 375 m grid, run as arrival times over the 24 h of D+1 with hourly BARRA, starting from the burnt area on D; arrival time gives the score. Assumptions:
  - Forest fuel hazard scores and near-surface height come from time since last burnt, via standard fuel accumulation curves.
  - Fuel-class mapping: forest, woodland and pine plantations → Vesta (dry eucalypt forest); shrubland/heath → mallee-heath model; grassland and cropland → CSIRO grassland model.
  - Grass curing follows a seasonal curve (no daily curing data).
  - Slope: ROS × e^(0.069 θ). Elliptical fire shape with length-to-breadth from wind speed. No spotting, no firefighting.
  - One global rate-of-spread scale factor, tuned in the season CV; everything else fixed from the literature.

- Finding (fold 4, 2026-11-05): fold 4 (2021+2024) is a grass fold: 92% of its new burn is grass/crop (other folds 7–20%), and grass/crop scores badly everywhere (active-fire persistence F1 0.02–0.09 vs 0.22–0.32 in forest). It's dominated by one "event", 30310 (2021, Western District, −37.85°, 143.30°): 72% of 2021's labelled cells, active on and off from 2 Mar to 9 May 2021, 6,849 cells (~257 kha). It looks like autumn burn-offs chained together by 5 km / 14 d linking, not one bushfire. It's tagged "deeca_bushfire" because 7 of its 1,999 detections (0.35%) touch a DEECA bushfire outline, which also exempts it from the cropland rule (and the fuel map calls the land grassland, not cropland). Its MCD64A1 outline is strong (ratio 3.8), so the low score isn't weak labels, but there's more uncertain area around it (uncertain cells per labelled cell: fold 4 11.9 vs 5–8 elsewhere). 37 of the 589 "deeca_bushfire" events (6%, 10k detections) have <25% of their detections inside DEECA bushfire outlines.

- 2026-11-05: Event tagging fix. An event is "deeca_bushfire" only if ≥ 0.375 of its detections fall inside DEECA bushfire outlines (`DEECA_MIN_SHARE`), the empty bin (0.35–0.40) in the histogram of that share over the 592 tagged events (455 are ≥ 0.95; the gap is narrow). 52 events were re-tagged to unmatched; most big ones are Western District burn-off chains. Exception: NSW Black Summer fire 26913 (2020, Kosciuszko, 30% inside DEECA outlines), which is kept as unmatched. Re-tagged events that touch planned-burn outlines become planned burns (13; mostly genuine, 50–95% inside burn outlines).
- 2026-11-05: Stop-start rule (`bursty_unmatched`, unmatched events only): ≥3 bursts of activity separated by quiet gaps > 3 days, with no burst holding a majority (largest burst < 50%). Drops 361 events. The "no dominant burst" condition is what protects real fires seen through smoke: NSW Black Summer fire 26814 has 3 bursts but its largest burst holds 100% (with gaps alone it would have been dropped), and 26913 has 2 bursts. No 2019/2020 event with ≥300 detections is dropped. There's no natural gap in the largest-burst share; 50% is a principled majority rule.

## Test protocol (approved by Rishi 2026-11-06; nothing has touched test yet)
Run once (`python -m vigil.test_eval`; refuses to run if `docs/results/test_results.csv` exists), committed and reported whatever the result. No change to models, settings or thresholds after seeing it.
- **Models:** (1) main: U-Net no weather + physics; (2) plain U-Net; (3) U-Net + physics; (4) best baseline: wind-shifted persistence from active fire; (5) physics alone.
- **Training:** U-Nets on all 12 non-test seasons (all cached CV windows), 3 seeds each, reported as mean ± seed sd. Settings come from CV only:
  - fire weight / no-burn rate: the modal per-fold CV choice; tie → best mean CV F1;
  - epochs: fixed = median CV early-stop epoch (no inner-val fold is left for early stopping). Early-stop epoch = epochs run − patience (6); runs that hit the 30-epoch cap count as 30 (an upper bound; their best lies in 25–30). Median over the 15 CV runs (5 folds × 3 seeds) of that model;
  - threshold: median of the 15 CV thresholds (each chosen on its inner-val fold);
  - normalisation: the "all" stats in `norm_stats.json`.
  Baseline: modal CV variant and cutoff (wind smear 0.25 cells per m/s, 0.38 km). Physics alone: modal CV ROS scale, predicted = reached within 24 h; physics CV is rerun on the fixed data first (`physics.cv`), since its CV numbers predate the edge mask. Physics channel at scale 1, already computed for 2020.
- **Data:** season 2020, the same windows, masks and scoring as CV (whole fires + 24 km margin, edge mask, already-burnt cells excluded, far cells kept and scored in the >24 km band).
- **Metrics:** F1 (headline), IoU, precision, recall, PR-AUC, pooled over all test fire-days; F1/recall by distance band (≤6, 6–24, >24 km); F1/precision/recall by fuel group (a cell's dominant fuel: forest/woodland incl. plantation; shrub/heath; grass/crop; other = wetland/urban/non-burnable); each fire separately (for the website).
- **Uncertainty:** 95% bootstrap CIs by resampling test fires (2,000 resamples, seed-mean metrics per resample). Paired bootstrap (same resampled fires for both models) for main − plain U-Net and main − best baseline, F1 and PR-AUC, with 95% CIs on the difference and the share of resamples > 0.
- **CV alongside:** every test number is reported next to the same model's CV score (mean over folds), and the differences next to their CV differences.
- **Check before running:** the new scorer reproduces recorded CV scores exactly on fold 0 (U-Net F1 0.3470, U-Net + physics 0.3735, baseline 0.2729), and a dry run (`--dry-run 0`) exercises the whole script on 3 CV fires.
- **Expectation, written in advance:** 2020 is more extreme than any training season (5.8% of new burn > 24 km vs 0.5% in train), so absolute scores may fall below CV; the comparison between models is the main result.
- **Run order (`data/processed/logs/test_chain.sh`):** tuning finishes → physics CV → dry run incl. physics → test, strictly one after another, stopping at the first failure.
